Tutto.Ground · the two stories
Ground

Two stories about AI, both wrong

You've been sold fear or hype. The people who shouted "it takes every job" the loudest are the ones now walking it back.

The straight version: AI is a real shift, some work changes, and it opens up things that used to need money or years of training. The answer to a changing job is a new skill, not a redundant person.

03
Tutto.Ground · what it is
Ground — if you take one thing away

A calculator is certain. AI guesses well.

Software follows fixed rules: 2 + 2 is 4, every time. AI predicts the most likely next word — brilliant for language, but it can be confidently wrong.

Claude understands your language and turns it into the instructions. The instructions do the work, the same way each time. That pairing is the whole trick you're about to watch.

Tutto.Show · live
Show — leave the deck now

Empty folder to a working tool

A village carpenter's quote system, built in front of you. Three steps: the job files, the workshop's rules, and a tool that turns one into a devis — the same devis, every time.

Full script in demo/demo-script.md. If the wifi drops, open the pre-built folder and still run the tool live — the point survives offline.

05
Tutto.Show · what you saw
Show

The AI did the small job. The rules did the work.

That's the pattern behind everything worth building: the machine reads and phrases, your written-down rules decide, and the result holds still.

About twenty dollars a month, on your own laptop. The hard part isn't the tool — it's writing the rules for the work that actually matters, and owning the loop of fixing them until they hold.

Tutto.You · your work
You

The same pattern fits most of your week

A folder of documents, a set of rules, a steady result. That's a quote, an enquiry, a form, a set of minutes — most of the paperwork that eats your time.

You don't put your work into an app. You put the tool into your own folder, on your own machine, and it learns how you do things.

07
Tutto.You · your children
You

We've done this before — the calculator

Teachers picketed. "Children will never learn to think." It didn't destroy mathematics. It became ordinary — but only because the teaching changed alongside it.

AI won't become ordinary on its own — it can be confidently wrong, so the skill our children need is directing it and checking it, not fearing it or trusting it blindly. That teaching is exactly what tonight is a small piece of.

08
Tutto.Control · safely
Control

Safety is a dial, and you hold it

The postcard versus the sealed envelope. How much leaves your hands is a setting you choose — and the way you build is the same all the way up.

One safe-use habit covers most of it: before you type something in, ask "would I mind if this exact text were public?" If yes, move up the dial.

09
Tutto.Control · sovereign
Control

"Where it sits" isn't the same as "who controls it"

This matters here more than most places. A French data centre owned by a foreign company still answers to that country's law. Location is not control.

You don't need the top of the dial for a devis. You do need to know the choice exists — and that with your own tools, on your own machine, the choice is yours.

Tutto.Control · don't look away
Control

The one thing worse than getting it wrong

Nobody here has to become technical. But looking away isn't safety — it's the only move that leaves you with no say in it.

You are not meant to absorb all of this tonight. You saw one thing work, once. That's the point. Being early, in a village, with your own tools, is the whole advantage.
