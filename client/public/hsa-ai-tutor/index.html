A tutor that doesn't give the answer

Laetitia asked whether Claude can tutor the way Khan Academy's Khanmigo does: ask the learner questions until they get there themselves. It can. Most of it you can write once with Claude and reuse for every learner, which costs nothing each time a learner gets stuck.

The hint ladder

Each wrong answer gets a hint that says a little more than the last. The learner only sees the worked answer after three tries, and even then it explains why and points back to the guide.

Three ways to build it

Start with the first. It covers most of what a tutor does, and it costs nothing per learner because the hints are written in advance.

1 · Written hints in Moodle quizzes

Claude writes three hints for every question from the learner guide. You check them, then they go into the Moodle question bank. Moodle's "interactive with multiple tries" setting shows a hint after each wrong try.

Cost per learner: none
2 · Branching scenarios

A short case where each choice leads somewhere different: the warm box goes on the shelf, and the next screen shows what happens. Built as an artifact or in H5P, which Moodle runs.

Cost per learner: none
3 · A live AI tutor

A chat window where learners ask anything about the module. Most like Khanmigo, and the only one that answers questions you didn't predict. Each message is charged through the API, and it needs firm rules.

Cost per learner: every message

Our advice: build 1 for Stock Receiving first, add 2 where a scenario teaches better than a question, and pilot 3 with one cohort once you know which questions learners actually ask. Make it once explains the cost side.

Rules a live tutor follows

If you pilot a live tutor, these go into its instructions. They're the same things you'd tell a new tutor on their first day.

  • Never the answer first. Ask one question at a time and wait for the learner.
  • Only HSA material. It answers from the learner guide and resource guide for the module, and says so when something isn't in them.
  • Three tries, then explain. The same ladder as above, so learners don't get stuck and give up.
  • Hand over to a person. Anything about marks, complaints or personal problems goes to the facilitator.
  • No personal details. Learners don't type ID numbers or health information, and the tutor never asks for them.
  • A spending cap. A monthly limit is set before learners get access, and someone looks at the bill every month.
