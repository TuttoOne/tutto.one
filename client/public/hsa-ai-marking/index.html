Marking with AI and a moderator

Laetitia asked how far Claude could go in marking a learner's online knowledge portfolio against the rubric. As a first marker, quite far. A registered assessor still signs every result, and moderation only comes down when the numbers show it's safe. The biggest gain is the one Laetitia named: every learner is marked to the same standard.

For Laetitia and Christine, then the assessors Status an idea to pilot and prove before anyone approves it

Who does what

Claude reads and suggests. People decide. Nothing goes to the gradebook without an assessor's name on it.

Brief it like a new assessor

Laetitia's own answer: if you hired a pharmacist to mark, you'd rely on what they already know and write down the rest. Claude needs the same written brief. The more you write down, the less it guesses.

What a pharmacist brings
  • What stock receiving, cold chain and schedules are
  • The vocabulary of a pharmacy
  • Judgement about whether an answer "means the same"

Claude has most of the general knowledge but none of your judgement, so the brief has to spell it out.

What you write down
  • The rubric and the memo for each question
  • Answers that mean the same in other words, with examples
  • Common mistakes and how to mark them
  • What never passes, however well written
  • When to flag instead of marking

How much to check, and when to check less

Today you moderate 25%. Start above that, and come down only when Claude and your assessors agree, script after script.

What it gives you, and what it can't do

Gives you
  • The same standard for every learner, whoever the assessor is
  • Faster feedback, so learners resubmit sooner
  • A written reason for every mark, which helps with appeals
  • Flags on copying and on answers the rubric didn't foresee
Can't do
  • Sign a result or take responsibility for it
  • Mark well against a rubric that's vague, so the rubric gets better first
  • Always resist a confident, wrong answer: that's why people mark some portfolios blind
  • Replace the practical assessments in class

Making the case to the Council

Laetitia's fair worry: convincing the Pharmacy Council is a different matter. Go with evidence from a pilot.

  1. Talk earlyAn informal conversation with the Council and QCTO before the pilot, to hear their concerns.
  2. Pilot one moduleEvery portfolio marked by Claude and by an assessor, without either seeing the other's mark.
  3. Show the numbersHow often they agreed, where they didn't and what changed in the brief as a result.
  4. Show the controlsWho signs, the moderation plan, the blind samples, appeals unchanged, POPIA and where learner work is processed.

Running cost. Marking a new portfolio is live work, so it goes through the API and is charged per portfolio. The pilot tells you the real cost per portfolio before anything is decided. See Make it once.

Prompts to copy

Practise on made-up or fully anonymised portfolios until IT and FPD have agreed where real learner work can go.

1 · Write the assessor briefHere is the rubric and memo for the Stock Receiving knowledge portfolio, and 5 marked examples with the assessor's comments. Write assessor-brief.md for a new assessor who is a pharmacist but has never marked for us: for each question, what earns the mark, answers that mean the same in other words, common mistakes, what never passes, and when to flag it for a person instead of marking. Quote the examples. List anything the rubric leaves unclear.
2 · Compare first marks with the assessor'sUsing @assessor-brief.md, mark these 10 anonymised portfolios. For each question give the mark, one line on why, and any flag. Then compare with @assessor-marks.xlsx: where did we disagree, which of us followed the brief, and what should change in the brief? Don't change the assessor's marks.
