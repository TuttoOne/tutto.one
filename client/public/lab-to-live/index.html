Where the team lab runs

As soon as a tool is for more than one person, it needs somewhere shared to run. Two ways to do it, and the code lives in your GitHub organisation either way.

A private server online

A server rented in Europe, only reachable with your team's logins. Paid monthly.

  • Works from home, other offices and on the road
  • Nothing to buy, easy to grow
  • Your documents sit with a hosting company, so it goes in your data records

A machine in the office

A box such as an NVIDIA DGX Spark or a Mac Studio, on your own network. It can run AI models itself. Bought once.

  • Sensitive documents never leave the building
  • The whole team shares one machine
  • Hardware to buy and a cupboard to lock

The check before anything goes live

Five questions. A tool goes live when all five have a yes and someone has signed for it.

  1. Data. What does it read, where does that go and who can see it? Does it match your AI charter?
  2. Security. Logins for each person, no passwords or keys inside the code, and only the right people can change it.
  3. Connections. If it touches an older system (accounts, stock, the CRM), it has been tested on a copy first and it reads before it ever writes.
  4. It does the job. It passes its scorecard on real examples, checked by the people who'll use it.
  5. An owner and a way back. One named person looks after it, and if something goes wrong we can switch back to the last good version in minutes.

Who signs it off

You have an IT team

Your IT team signs

We prepare the sign-off pack with the team (what the tool does, the five answers, the test results) so IT can review it quickly and say yes or ask for changes.

You don't

I sign, with a specialist where needed

I take responsibility for the sign-off. When a tool needs more than I can check, I bring in a specialist in security or data protection, or someone who knows your older systems inside out.

The person who signs is never someone who built the tool. They come to it fresh from outside the team and check what it does with real information before your customers see it.

What we set up with you

Building on laptops is where every team starts, and it works for one person. The moment a tool is shared, it needs a proper home.

Shared without a team lab

  • The tool runs on one laptop and stops when that person is away
  • Copies get emailed round and nobody knows which one is right
  • A quick fix goes straight to the people using it
  • IT finds out last and says no

With a team lab and a check

  • Everyone uses the same version, from wherever they work
  • Every change has a name, a reason and a way back
  • Testing happens where customers can't see it
  • IT gets a pack they can say yes to

  • Team lab set-up

    We choose the server or office machine with you, set up your GitHub organisation, logins and data rules, then move your first tool in.

    Quoted once
