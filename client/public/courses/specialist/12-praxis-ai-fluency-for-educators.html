Tutto.Lesson 1 · Foundation
Lesson 1

Context first — write it once, reuse it everywhere

AI gets useful when it knows your situation. Spend the first session turning your teaching into one reusable document.

Ten minutes of self-reflection first — values, constraints, who your students are, the methods you trust — then let Claude interview you and synthesise it into one document. Every later conversation starts already onboarded.

03
Tutto.Lesson 2 · Foundation
Lesson 2 — if you take one thing away

The 4Ds — how you collaborate, not just what you ask

Augmentation, not automation: you stay in the work. Four habits, each with three parts — and a loop in the middle that turns a tool into a thinking partner.

Describe, then discern, then describe again — that continuous loop is the engine. Delegation sets it up; Diligence keeps you responsible for accuracy, bias and transparency. (Full breakdown: Anthropic's AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations.)

04
Tutto.Lesson 3 · In practice
Lesson 3

Designing the course — you hold the vision

Plan, set the context, then work the loop across three design tasks — and document what you decided and why.

For every suggestion, ask "does this serve my students?" and explain your reasoning back — build on good ideas, redirect weak ones. Ask the AI to take the students' view at transition points: what might confuse them, what scaffolding is missing.

05
Tutto.Lesson 4 · In practice
Lesson 4

One loop builds anything students use

Same three steps for every material: set the task on top of your context, refine in the loop, then run the Diligence check before it reaches a student.

Established context is what makes materials cohere — slides, guide and quiz line up because they share one source. Academic integrity cuts both ways: when students have AI too, fluency helps you design assessments that ask for authentic learning.
