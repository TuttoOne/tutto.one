Tutto.Lesson 1 · Meet Cowork
Lesson 1

Chat thinks with you. Cowork does the work.

Three modes of Claude on the desktop, built for three shapes of work. The shift that matters: you hand Cowork an outcome, not a question.

Chat is where you go to think out loud. Cowork is for the work you'd normally do yourself: describe an outcome and Claude plans, executes, and delivers the whole thing — context-gathering, analysis, file production, tool use.

03
Tutto.Lesson 2 · Meet Cowork
Lesson 2

Point it at a folder. That folder is the boundary.

Three setup choices: the working folder Claude reads and writes, the connectors that reach your apps, and how closely it asks before acting.

Install the desktop app, sign in, pick Cowork. The folder is the single most important choice — pick the smallest one that holds what the task needs. Cloud connectors are often read-only; your local folder is where Claude builds and iterates.

Tutto.Lesson 3 · Meet Cowork
Lesson 3

Three shapes of work worth handing off

When a task is multi-step, lives in real files, or spans your tools, give it to Cowork. The patterns tell you what to delegate.

The through-line: work that used to mean juggling steps, files and tools in your head can now be handed off as a single task. Scheduled tasks and Dispatch only run when your computer is on and Claude is running.

05
Tutto.Lesson 4 · Meet Cowork
Lesson 4 — if you take one thing away

Brief it like a smart colleague

A good prompt names the deliverable, the inputs, and the nuance Claude can't guess. Then you answer its questions, steer if it drifts, and review the result.

More of the back-and-forth happens upfront, before Claude starts, instead of across five rounds of "actually, can you also…". The deliverable is the artifact, not the chat — check that it met the objective and that nothing's made up.

Tutto.Lesson 5 · Make it yours
Lesson 5

Four ways Cowork gets better at your work

Each one gives Claude a different kind of knowledge to carry forward — so the context you supply once keeps paying off.

You won't start with all four on day one. Set global instructions first, add a project as recurring work surfaces, build a skill when you're tired of re-explaining a process, and bundle a plugin once you have something worth sharing.

07
Tutto.Lesson 6 · Make it yours
Lesson 6

Standing context: about you, and about the work

Global instructions cover you, in every session. A project covers one stream of work — and quietly builds its own memory as you go.

That last one is the difference projects make. Outside a project each session starts fresh; inside one, every conversation adds to what Claude knows — so the next task opens with the client's situation and last week's decisions already in hand.

Tutto.Lesson 7 · Make it yours
Lesson 7

A skill is a playbook Claude loads when the task matches

A folder of files that teaches Claude how to do one kind of work the way you'd want it done. Up to four parts — only the first is required.

Instructions tell Claude what to do; assets give it the raw materials; references show it what "good" looks like; scripts run the repeatable parts the same way every time. Include what the work needs, nothing more.

09
Tutto.Lesson 8 · Make it yours
Lesson 8

A plugin bundles skills around a job

Where a skill is one playbook, a plugin is several — plus the connectors and subagents they need. It comes in two shapes.

Anthropic publishes plugins for common roles — finance, legal, sales, support. Install one off the shelf, customise it to your templates, or build your own. The expertise travels with the install, not the person. Start with /setup-cowork to find the right one.

Tutto.Lesson 9 · Everywhere you work
Lesson 9

Claude in Chrome — the bridge for tools with no connector

Dashboards, vendor portals, web apps behind a login. Claude reads and acts on the page itself, then hands the results back to Cowork.

You stay signed in — Claude works in the session you've already authenticated, it can't log in for you. One conversation, both surfaces. (Claude in Chrome may not be available on some enterprise plans.)

Tutto.Lesson 10 · Everywhere you work
Lesson 10

Claude for Microsoft 365 — inside the document

Claude shows up as an add-in in Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook. One conversation carries context from one app to the next.

Reach for Cowork when work pulls from many sources and ends in a deliverable; reach for Claude in M365 when you're editing the Office file in place. The two hand off cleanly — and the file is the file in both.

Tutto.Lesson 11 · Share & stay safe
Lesson 11

The folder sets the boundary; the prompt sets the bounds

Cowork always asks before deleting — that's the floor. These are the pre-emptive moves you bring on top.

Most mistakes don't happen because a safeguard failed — they happen because someone clicked through a confirmation that wasn't quite the action they intended. The dialog exists because the action matters; treat it that way.

13
Tutto.Lesson 12 · Share & stay safe
Lesson 12

An eval is a try-out: with the skill, and without

Before a skill leaves your desk, see it side by side against the baseline. No code, no test scripts — just your judgment.

Built into skill-creator. The bar for shipping isn't perfect evals — it's that the cases you care about pass meaningfully better than the baseline, and you've named the ones you don't yet handle. Change one thing at a time.

14
Tutto.Lesson 13 · Share & stay safe
Lesson 13

From one laptop to the whole team

A skill that started as one person's way of working becomes something a teammate can install — through your org's private marketplace.

Teammates see it in their Directory, labeled as coming from your company; your updates flow to them automatically. Keep it healthy: one named owner, evals before every publish, name skills specifically, review quarterly.
