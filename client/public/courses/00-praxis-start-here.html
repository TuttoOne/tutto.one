Tutto.The idea · how it works
The idea

Software is exact. AI is likely. Pair them.

Software does what you tell it, the same way, every time. AI is different: it gives you answers based on probability, and it comes out a little different each time. Claude Code puts the two together. You say what you want in English, and it writes rules that then run the same way every time.

Think of it as an Excel formula that now lives in your folder. The logic is captured once, and it repeats.

03
Tutto.The idea · the architecture
The idea

Every system I build has the same four parts

Everything lives in a folder on your machine. The rules are written in plain English, the know-how is saved as a skill, a script does the repeating, and your files sit right next to them. You work in your own folders, not in the Claude app.

There's no website to go down and no portal to break. Everything the system knows is in that folder, and the folder is yours.

04
Tutto.The idea · the move
The idea

Don't ask for the answer. Ask for the thing that makes it.

If you ask AI for an answer, you get a good one, but it's different every time. If you ask it to build the script that produces the answer, you now have something that runs the same way for the next thousand. That's the difference between using AI and building with it.

This one move is behind every system on the next few screens.

The proof

Built, not bought. Starting with my own company.

I run my own company on systems built this way: our books and events, and our outreach. The subscriptions they replaced are cancelled. We build the same way for clients. I'll show you three, and why they keep getting better.

The best proof I have is that my own business runs on this. Two of these systems are mine, one we built for a client, and all three improve with use.

Tutto.The proof · my own company
Real system · Tutto's own books

My company's books and events, with no subscription

An event or a job becomes a quote. The quote becomes an invoice. It goes out through our own email service and the books stay current. I cancelled the accounting package; this does the job and the data stays with me.

No portal to log into, no monthly fee. We built it once and it runs every month.

Tutto.The proof · client outreach
Real system · TSplus outreach & intelligence

Sales that knows how to talk across borders

An emailing and intelligence system with its own dashboard. It drafts messages suited to how people communicate in each country, tracks the pipeline, and produces the reports and quotes I send straight to customers.

The country know-how is written into the rules, so every message goes out the way it should, however many we send.

Tutto.The proof · creative at scale
Real build · creative production for a client

One spec PDF in. Seventy-eight finished assets out.

An e-commerce platform sends a spec for 78 asset formats. A script turns that PDF into a reusable set of rules, like one big spreadsheet formula, then produces all 78. Each comes back editable, so a person finishes the last ten percent.

The manual part, reading a long spec and making every size by hand, becomes a formula you run.

09
Tutto.The proof · why it compounds
The proof

It gets better the more you use it

Not all 78 are perfect the first time. But the user shows the system what it got wrong, and the rules improve. Ten percent to fix becomes five percent, then one. The time you save grows every month.

This is the real return. A bought tool is as good as it will ever be on day one. A system you teach keeps improving.

Tutto.Your turn · scale & safety
Your turn

One build becomes many. And it can stay private.

Build it once for one case and you've built it for every case like it, whether that's ten or a hundred. And when the data is sensitive, the whole system can run on your own machine, where nothing leaves your network.

The work you do once pays back on every case like it. And when the system runs on your own machine, the data never leaves the office, so confidentiality is a fact, not a promise.

11
Tutto.Your turn · where to start
Your turn

How to find the one that's yours

You don't need a big strategy. Look for a task that ticks three boxes: it touches several systems, it repeats the same way every time, and it's costly when it's wrong. A task that ticks all three is where to start, and you can usually find one in an afternoon.

What it costs: about twenty dollars a month. Expect it to get you ninety percent of the way; you finish the last ten by hand. The hard thing to find is a guide who has done it before, not the software.
