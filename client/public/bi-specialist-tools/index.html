Find where a picture appears

Alice's everyday case: a profile picture on a selling platform like Vinted, and you want to know where else it's been used. You already use Google Lens in Chrome. Add three more engines and you'll find a lot more, because each one has its own index.

Google Lenscrop one thing at a time

TinEyesort by oldest
Log each hitlink and date found, like any source
  1. Google Lens. In Chrome, right-click the picture and choose Search with Google. Drag the box to crop to the face, then the background, then an object. Each crop gives you different results.
  2. Yandex and Bing. Upload the same file at yandex.com/images and bing.com/visualsearch.
  3. TinEye. At tineye.com, sort the results by Oldest. The first time a picture appeared online is often the most useful thing you'll find: a stock photo, a stolen picture or the person's first account.

Photos on Vinted, Instagram or Facebook usually have their hidden details (camera, date, GPS) removed. A photo a client sends you as an original file may still have them. Check it on your own computer, so the file stays with you. On a Mac, open it in Preview and choose Tools, Show Inspector. On Windows, right-click the file, Properties, Details.

Where the two AIs agree, start there. Where they don't, you have more places to check. The AI's guess never goes in the report. The side-by-side with the matching features marked does.

Prompt for ChatGPT and Gemini Where could this photo have been taken? First list every clue you can see: rock type, plants, colour of the water, the light and the sun, buildings, boats, anything written. Then give me your three best guesses, each with the clues that support it and how sure you are. I need places to check, so don't give me just one answer.
When you have a candidate I think this photo was taken at <place>. What should I look for in Google Earth or Street View to confirm it or rule it out? List the features in the photo that should match.

Use ChatGPT with thinking switched on. The AI tools sometimes refuse to locate a photo of a private person or their home. Upload only pictures you're allowed to share with that company, and check the training setting on any account you use for work.

Faces: check before you search

There are search engines that look for a face rather than a picture, such as PimEyes and FaceCheck.ID. They work, and in Italy they're a legal risk for you.

Picture search

Finds copies of that one picture. Use it freely, within your AI policy.

Face search

Finds the person in other photos. That's biometric data, a special category under GDPR. The Garante fined Clearview AI €20 million in 2022 for collecting faces of people in Italy.

Before anyone on the team uses a face search, agree when it's allowed, on what legal basis and who signs it off, and add that to your AI policy from session 1.

Cloud, Europe or your own machine

Three levels, for three kinds of work. Decide which level a job needs before you start it, the same way you'd decide who on the team should see a file.

  • Everyday: Claude

    Reports, proposals, translation, your instructions and scorecards. Most of your work stays here. Redacted case files, public sources, your templates.

  • Stays in Europe: Mistral

    A specialist that only reads the sensitive documents. You build the system with Claude. For a client who wants their data processed in the EU.

Never leaves the office: a machine of your own. For Alice's list of former employees, it works like this:
