The three steps that repeat everywhere

Whether it's a clip, a tutor, a question bank or a first mark, it's the same way of working you'd use with a new colleague.

  1. Write the briefWhat you want, for whom, from which source and what must never happen.
  2. Show what good looks likeA real example: a clip you like, a question exported from Moodle, a marked portfolio.
  3. A person signs it offNothing reaches a learner without someone's name on it.

For Alta: the narration rules file

The slow part of narrating a deck is writing the script with its pauses. Give Claude two or three scripts you've already made with their decks and have it write the rules down once. Then every new script follows them. Narration runs at about 130 to 150 words a minute, so a 20-second slide gets about 45 words: easy to check with a stopwatch.

1 · Write the rulesAttached are 3 narration scripts we've made and the slide decks they go with, for [narration program]. Write narration-rules.md: how and when pauses are marked and how long they are, how numbers, units, abbreviations and medicine names are written so the voice says them properly, South African English spelling, sentence length and tone, and roughly how many words a slide gets. Quote an example from my scripts for every rule.
2 · Write a new scriptUsing @narration-rules.md, write the narration for the attached deck, one section per slide with the slide number and title. Use only the slide and its speaker notes. Mark anything you added with [CHECK]. At the end, list every medicine name and abbreviation so I can check how the voice says them.
